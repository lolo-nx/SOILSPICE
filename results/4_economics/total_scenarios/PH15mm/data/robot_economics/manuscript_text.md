## Results

For daily robotic cleaning, the median total annual soiling costs are 3.58% (1.70–5.45%) in Almería, 3.32% (1.59–5.06%) in Doha, 4.79% (2.19–7.38%) in Neuchâtel of clean annual revenue (60 mm rectangular band, f=0.1, no additional glass margin, ground-market prices, PH15 mm scenario). Costs include remaining production losses, robot purchases/replacements and maintenance. Investment ranges are propagated at configuration level before taking the median over four modules and two orientations. The main case includes an explicit linear remaining-life credit in year 25. Without a terminal residual credit, the corresponding totals are 3.98% (1.88–6.08%) in Almería, 3.70% (1.76–5.64%) in Doha, 5.35% (2.45–8.25%) in Neuchâtel.

## Methods

All cleaning strategies are compared over a 25-year economic horizon at a 5% discount rate. The annual costs and clean revenues are held constant at their simulated ten-year mean values, as in the existing model. Robot initial investment is 2.4–8.2 EUR/m², converted using the existing 4.6 m²/kWp factor to 11.04–37.72 EUR/kWp. Robots are purchased at years 0, 12 and 24, with unchanged real purchase cost at each replacement. Maintenance and repair cost 5% of initial investment annually, with year-end payments in years 1–25. The present value of these costs is divided by the 25-year annuity factor (14.09394456604475) to obtain an equivalent annual ownership cost. This is added once to the simulated residual production-loss cost in both with-band and no-band cases. Manual cleaning fees are not applied to the daily robot. The main case uses a linear remaining-life credit of 11/12 of the year-24 robot purchase cost at year 25, discounted to year zero. This is an assumed service-value credit, not an observed or guaranteed resale value. Zero residual credit is evaluated separately as a sensitivity. No residual credit is assigned to the robots exhausted at replacement. No new energy, water, escalation or degradation assumptions are introduced.
