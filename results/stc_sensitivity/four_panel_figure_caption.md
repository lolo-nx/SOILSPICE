Effect of irradiance on relative power losses at a cell temperature of 25 °C. Panels show Yingli, SunPower, JA Solar and LONGi. Changes are calculated relative to the loss at 1000 W/m² for the same configuration. All 14 configurations per module are included: rectangular and triangular 80 mm bands at transmittances 0.71, 0.44 and 0, and a rectangular 60 mm band at transmittance 0.44, each in portrait and landscape. For the curves, the grey region indicates changes within ±2 percentage points and the horizontal line denotes zero change. For the heatmaps, row labels give shape, height (mm), orientation (P, portrait; L, landscape) and transmittance. Rect. denotes rectangular and Tri. denotes triangular. The difference is defined as ΔL(G) = L(G, 25 °C) − L(1000, 25 °C), in percentage points; negative values indicate lower relative losses than at the reference irradiance. The final two rows show the annual reference geometry, a 60 mm rectangle, tested here at τI = 0.44. Annual calculations use evolving band transmittances rather than a fixed value of 0.44. Heatmap values are printed where the absolute change exceeds 2 percentage points. The colour scale is shared and centred on zero. Of 224 conditions below 1000 W/m², 208 are within 2 percentage points of the reference. The largest change is −10.44 percentage points for SunPower in portrait with an opaque triangular 80 mm band at 200 W/m².
